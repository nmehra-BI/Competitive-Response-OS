    Five questions every response must answer

    Teams find competitor news in many places. The hard part is what comes next. The response case answers each question in one place, with a record of who did what.

    1. What changed, and how do we know?EvidenceEach claim links to its source, date and limits.
    2. Where does it affect our products, accounts, markets or opportunities?ImpactConfirmed product overlap and scoped exposure.
    3. What responses are reasonable, with what trade-offs?Response optionsBenefit, effort, risk and evidence for each option.
    4. Who approved what, and who will execute it by when?Decision and action planAn approved package version and owned tasks.
    5. What happened, and should we reassess the decision?OutcomesA scheduled review against a baseline.

      The case calculates exposure from a dated data snapshot. Each measure keeps its own definition. A scenario is an assumption that a person sets. It is not a prediction.

      AI proposes. People decide.

      These rules are part of the product. A team can trust the case record because the system cannot skip them.

      • Humans decideAI drafts analysis, options and tasks. Only an authorized person approves a response or records an outcome.
      • Approval binds to one versionAn approval applies to an exact package version. A material change makes a new version and pauses unsent work.
      • Confirmed means confirmedThe product shows "Confirmed" only after Jira returns an issue key. It never says "synced" before that.
      • Retries never duplicateA retry sends only the failed tasks, with the same idempotency key. After a timeout, the system checks Jira first.
      • Missing is never zeroWhen data is missing, the case shows "Exposure unavailable" and the reason. It never shows €0.
      • Restricted data stays restrictedRestricted values never appear in totals, tooltips, exports or AI context. The case does not show a count of hidden items.

      Prove one workflow, then expand

      Autonomy grows first for reversible analysis and drafts. Pricing, customer communication and consequential commercial changes always need explicit human authorization.

      1. Phase 1

        Discovery and concierge validation

        Interviews, past-case reconstruction, manual end-to-end trials, data and licensing assessment.Exit whenRepeated workflow pain, an identifiable buyer, and usable evidence and data.
      2. Phase 2, the pilot

        V1 paid-pilot MVP

        About 10 to 12 weeks after discovery and access approval (estimate)One segment and event family, watchlists, curated and manual intake, evidence, CSV exposure, one bounded AI assistant, options, approval, internal tasks, one Jira write path, manual outcome review.Exit whenTeams complete real cases and show repeatable workflow benefit.
      3. Phase 3

        V2 specialization

        Read-only CRM, stronger ingestion, domain skill library, specialist agents only where evaluations justify them, Teams notifications, richer monitoring.Exit whenMeasurable quality or efficiency gains over V1.
      4. Phase 4

        V3 policy-controlled autonomy

        Auto-refresh of evidence, drafted follow-ups, low-risk internal actions under explicit policies.Exit whenSustained evaluations, recoverability, security and operational evidence.
